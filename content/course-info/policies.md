## Attendance, Zoom, and Recordings

You may attend over Zoom as much as you'd like. All classes are recorded, and
recordings are released immediately. Recitations are recommended but optional.
You are responsible for any material covered in class.

Two honest caveats about attending remotely:

- Lectures pause for questions frequently, and Zoom is checked much less often,
  if at all. If you are someone who likes to ask questions during class, Zoom is
  probably not a good exclusive attendance mechanism for you.
- Zoom and recordings occasionally break. This is not meant to scare you into
  attending in person, and things work the majority of the time, but for
  concrete numbers from the last offering: one lecture lost its audio recording
  entirely, one lecture had the in-room mic die halfway through and finished on
  a laptop mic, and screen sharing is occasionally missed when switching between
  tutorial Jupyter notebooks.

## Late Submissions

You have a total of **ten (10) late days** to use across your homeworks, divided
however you like, no questions asked. Before you have used all ten, there is no
need to tell us that you are using them, or why.

Once your ten late days are consumed, if we do not hear from you, our default
position is to assign no credit to late homeworks. The general philosophy here
is to be flexible within reason. That said, if you keep slipping further behind
past the allotted ten days as the semester goes on, we will probably have a
conversation before you continue receiving credit.

## Collaboration

You are encouraged to collaborate on homeworks. We expect each student to
(1) submit solutions written in their own words and their own code, and
(2) indicate with whom they collaborated.

A good rule of thumb: meet with your collaborators, discuss the problems, then
part ways for at least an hour before sitting down to write up final solutions
from whatever you sketched out together.

There is **no collaboration permitted on exams**.

## Use of AI Language Models

The use of AI language models (ChatGPT and similar) **is permitted** in this
class. Two factors decided this: such tools are currently impossible to forbid
effectively, and forbidding them would be ironic at best in a machine learning
course. A few caveats:

- **Cite them.** List ChatGPT as a collaborator, along with a brief (1 to 2
  sentence) summary of how you used it. Their use is explicitly allowed, so this
  costs you nothing, and it may help you when we assign partial credit.
- **Buyer beware.** ChatGPT is excellent at being confidently wrong, and that
  nonsense tends to look different from the mistakes students make while
  learning. If your solutions are obviously copies of confidently incorrect
  output, we will not be particularly sympathetic about partial credit.
- **Plagiarism is still plagiarism.** These models occasionally reproduce text
  wholesale from popular sources like textbooks. You remain responsible for the
  academic honesty policy, and not knowing the model was plagiarizing will earn
  minimal sympathy.
- **It will cost you on exams.** If you lean on an AI companion too heavily, you
  may find yourself at a significant disadvantage during exams and oral
  assessments. Homework is meant to challenge you enough that exam questions
  feel comparatively easy.

## Academic Integrity

Violations of Penn's [Code of Academic Integrity](https://catalog.upenn.edu/pennbook/code-of-academic-integrity/) will be reported to the Office of Student Conduct. When in doubt, ask before submitting.

## Accommodations

Students requesting accommodations should contact the [Weingarten Learning Resources Center](https://www.vpul.upenn.edu/lrc/) and forward their letter to the instructor as early in the semester as possible.
