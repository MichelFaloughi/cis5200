## Homework 0

Homework 0 is released in the second week of classes. It exists to give you a
sense of the level of mathematics you should be comfortable with to get the most
out of CIS 5200, and it is **only checked for completion**.

You should still take it seriously and use it as a tool to gauge how comfortable
you will be for the rest of the course. Language models will trivially solve
every problem on Homework 0, so it can take as little time as you'd like, but
confirming that an LLM can do it will not help you on the exams or the oral
assessments. Machine learning requires a broad understanding across
multivariable calculus, linear algebra, probability and statistics, programming,
and algorithms.

If you are comfortable with the material in Homework 0, you will very likely do
well in this course. If you find yourself struggling with many parts of it, you
may find the remainder of the course quite challenging and may want to pick up
additional background first. Penn is piloting
[CIS 3990, Mathematics of Machine Learning](/course-info/similar-courses), which
is worth considering.

## Homeworks

Beyond Homework 0 there are **five homeworks** over the semester, with roughly
two weeks to complete each one. Together they are worth 20% of your grade.

Most homeworks have a programming component, completed in Jupyter notebooks
(Google Colab works well) using Python and PyTorch. Submissions go through
Gradescope.

Expect homework problems to challenge you in ways that require thinking a little
beyond the material covered in lecture. Homeworks carry the bulk of the
technical knowledge in this class, and that is intentional: you have a good
amount of time for each one.

Late days are covered under [Policies](/course-info/policies). Collaboration is
encouraged, subject to the collaboration policy on that same page.

## Exams

There is a **midterm** (25%) and a **final exam** in December (30%). See the
[schedule](/) for dates as they are confirmed.

Unlike homework questions, which are designed to be technical and to push you
past the course material, exams are meant to be relatively easier questions that
require less calculation and test your understanding of the high level concepts
covered in class. There is no collaboration permitted on exams.

## Oral Assessments

There are **two oral assessments**, worth 12.5% each, for 25% of your grade in
total. They take place in roughly weeks 8 and 12; see the [schedule](/) for
dates.

An oral assessment is a sit-down, interview-style session modeled on a machine
learning engineering interview. Your TA asks you machine learning questions and
grades your answers. The goal is to prepare you for real-world MLE interviews.

**Scheduling:** each TA has a Calendly link, which you use to book your slot
with your TA. If none of your TA's times work for you, email them and we will
find another TA who can take you.
