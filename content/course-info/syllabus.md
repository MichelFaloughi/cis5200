## Prerequisites

- Linear algebra (matrix operations, eigendecomposition)
- Multivariable calculus (gradients, partial derivatives)
- Probability & statistics (distributions, expectation, MLE)
- Programming proficiency in Python; experience with NumPy

Not sure whether you meet these? [Homework 0](/course-info/assessments) is
released in the second week and is designed to let you gauge exactly that. It is
graded for completion only. If much of it gives you trouble, consider picking up
additional background first, such as
[CIS 3990](/course-info/similar-courses).

## Topics Covered

- **Foundations:** the supervised learning setup, k-nearest neighbors, and the
  curse of dimensionality
- **Linear models and optimization:** the perceptron, gradient descent,
  empirical risk minimization, linear regression, and logistic regression
- **Margin-based and kernel methods:** linear support vector machines, nonlinear
  modeling with kernels, kernel SVMs, and Gaussian processes
- **Neural networks:** feedforward networks and transformers
- **Tree-based and ensemble methods:** decision trees, gradient boosted
  regression trees, AdaBoost, bagging, and random forests
- **Evaluating and debugging models:** the bias-variance decomposition, model
  debugging, and performance metrics
- **Unsupervised learning:** dimensionality reduction and PCA, k-means
  clustering, and Gaussian mixture models

See the [schedule](/) for the week by week breakdown. Topics are tentative and
may shift as the semester goes on.

## Recommended Reading

No single textbook is required. The following are useful references:

- *Pattern Recognition and Machine Learning* by Bishop
- *The Elements of Statistical Learning* by Hastie, Tibshirani, Friedman
- *Deep Learning* by Goodfellow, Bengio, Courville
